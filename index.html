Ordinary Animals of São Paulo

During the symposium we will arrange 2-hour walks departing from the Biblioteca Brasiliana at USP, looking and listening for the everyday fauna of the campus. This page is an introduction to what you can expect: species are presented with their common names in Portuguese and English, followed by their scientific name.

01Parrots

Leo Breder ↗

Maracanã-pequena

Red-shouldered Macaw|Diopsittaca nobilis

It is the smallest of the macaws, though it has long been placed outside of the genus Ara. It can be distinguished by the white skin on its face and two-toned beak. The name Maracanã, shared with the famous football stadium, has its origin in the indigenous Tupi word maraká, a rattle instrument, which its vocalizations resemble.

Leo Breder ↗

Periquito-rico

Plain Parakeet|Brotogeris tirica

Noisy little one, it vocalizes in flight and while foraging. Look out for its long and pointy tail and the blue on its wings.

Leo Breder ↗

Papagaio-verdadeiro

Turquoise-fronted Amazon|Amazona aestiva

The population established in the city is originated from individuals released from captivity. Every evening is easy to hear their hoarse “crau-crau” vocalizations, as they return to their roosting sites in the forest fragment on campus.

02Park dwellers

Bruno Luka ↗

Beija-flor-tesoura

Swallow-tailed Hummingbird|Eupetomena macroura

It is one of the largest hummingbirds in Brazil. Paulo loves to say that when naming these birds, the Portuguese language hit the jackpot of poetry. The translation “flower kisser” does not do justice to the hundreds of songs that allude to them.

Leo Breder ↗

Anu-branco

Guira Cuckoo|Guira guira

These highly social birds are usually seen in groups of 6 to 18 individuals. Common in open grasslands and scrub savanna, they are regarded by some Brazilian birders as a glimpse of what small dinosaurs may have looked like.

Augusto Nunes ↗

Alma-de-gato

Common Squirrel-Cuckoo|Piaya cayana

It is a large arboreal cuckoo that jumps from one tree branch to the another and can sometimes be seen crossing open areas with a mixture of gliding and wing-beating. It has a long tail, rufous body, greenish beak, and very distinctive red eyes encircled by bare red skin.

Leo Breder ↗

Anu-preto

Smooth-billed Ani|Crotophaga ani

Group foragers similar to G. guira, they can be seen hunting arthropods on the ground, but also in the nests of smaller birds. To our human eyes, their flight often appears as sloppy and ungraceful. The name Anu comes from the indigenous Tupi name anũ, given to the Brazilian Cuculidae.

Bruno Luka ↗

Quero-quero

Southern Lapwing|Vanellus chilensis

Not every living bird has claws on its wings, but this one has very prominent pink ones. Be careful when walking around campus, it has a record for being territorial. Its onomatopoeic name can be translated into English as “I want-I want”, and will surely be heard at USP.

Bruno Luka ↗

João-de-barro

Rufous Hornero|Furnarius rufus

The pinnacle of the ovenbirds in the family Furnariidae, it builds oven-shaped nests made of mud, a task shared by the couple. Its song is commonly a long, rhythmic duet that mixes sharp “kweep!” and slower “jeet!” notes.

Leo Breder ↗

Bem-te-vi

Great Kiskadee|Pitangus sulphuratus

Another onomatopoeic name, its call resembles a yelled “Bem-te-vi!”, but in English the call is described as “Kis-ka-dee!”.

Leo Breder ↗

Sabiá-laranjeira

Rufous-bellied Thrush|Turdus rufiventris

If you hear a bird singing in the middle of the night in São Paulo, it is probably this one. In a city where light and noise pollution are everywhere all the time, this species seems to have shifted the timing of its vocalizations from early morning to during the night. The official bird of the state.

Bruno Luka ↗

Sabiá-barranco

Pale-breasted Thrush|Turdus leucomelas

It has one of the grumpiest calls among the Thrushes in Brazil, even though its song is just as melodic.

Leo Breder ↗

Tico-tico

Rufous-collared Sparrow|Zonotrichia capensis

One of the most well-known birds in Brazil, due to its abundance, wide distribution, and mention in the song Tico-tico no fubá.

Leo Breder ↗

Sanhaço-cinzento

Sayaca Tanager|Thraupis sayaca

A less colorful tanager, that is still impressive. It is very active and vocal, and it is an important frugivore in small forest fragments and urban parks. It can commonly be seen in mixed flocks.

Leo Breder ↗

Cambacica

Bananaquit|Coereba flaveola

A classic at bird feeders and on ornamental flowers, it looks like a miniature version of the Bem-te-vi because of its coloration, but with a much more slender bill.

03Big frugivores

Leo Breder ↗

Jacu

Dusky-legged Guan|Penelope obscura

The largest avian frugivore of the Brazilian Atlantic Rainforest, it is not uncommonly spotted around chickens in rural and suburban areas. Usually unafraid of people, jacus walk around in small groups in the more forested parts of campus. It is a central species in many seed-dispersal networks.

Leo Breder ↗

Tucano-toco

Toco Toucan|Ramphastos toco

Simply amazing. Originally occurring in open habitats such as forest edges and savanna, it seems to be expanding its range and has recently established a local population in São Paulo. The largest of the toucans, it still has a relatively light mostly hollow beak, that is used to forage for fruits and even small vertebrates.

Leo Breder ↗

Tucano-de-bico-verde

Red-breasted Toucan|Ramphastos dicolorus

A smaller toucan, commonly observed in small groups in more forested areas of São Paulo. It feeds mostly on fruit and can be seen in the invasive king palm (Archontophoenix cunninghamiana) around campus.

04Native Pigeons and Doves

Leo Breder ↗

Asa-branca

Picazuro Pigeon|Patagioenas picazuro

Now abundant in urban and rural areas, is mentioned in a famous forró song that bears its name. To distinguish it from the Rock Pigeon (Columba livia), look at the sides of the neck, with iridescent scaly edges, and at its wings, with white edges, or white streaks visible when in flight.

Leo Breder ↗

Rolinha-caldo-de-feijão

Ruddy Ground Dove|Columbina talpacoti

A small, purply and brownish dove that receives its common name because of the similarity of its color with one of the main dishes in the country, boiled beans.

05Easy to hear, hard to find

Leo Breder ↗

Saracura-do-mato

Slaty-breasted Wood-Rail|Aramides saracura

Alone or in small groups of up to four individuals. Their calls can be heard in the early morning or in the evening in the forest fragment on campus, but they can be seen foraging along the day on the forest edge.

Leo Breder ↗

Pitiguari

Rufous-browed Peppershrike|Cyclarhis gujanensis

The hardest one to find, as it is small, cryptic, and occurs in the canopy of more forested areas. Nonetheless, its song is very distinctive and can be easily heard year-round.

Bruno Luka ↗

Corruíra

Southern House Wren|Troglodytes musculus

Once considered one of the passerines with the widest latitudinal range in the world, from Canada to Tierra del Fuego, it has now been split from the T. aedon group. Nonetheless, due to its behavioral flexibility, it is regularly found in cities.

06Wood-climbers

Leo Breder ↗

Pica-pau-branco

White Woodpecker|Melanerpes candidus

Usually in groups, they raid bee and wasp nests, and roost together. The least woodpecker-colored of the woodpeckers.

Leo Breder ↗

Pica-pau-do-campo

Campo Flicker|Colaptes campestris

Commonly in groups, this woodpecker spends a significant part of its life on the ground. In rural areas, it is commonly seen on termite nests.

Leo Breder ↗

Pica-pau-de-cabeça-amarela

Blond-crested Woodpecker|Celeus flavescens

Usually solitary or in pairs, this woodpecker is mostly seen in more forested areas, foraging on tree trunks, and not only for insects, as it regularly feeds on fruit.

Augusto Nunes ↗

Pica-pau-de-banda-branca

Lineated Woodpecker|Dryocopus lineatus

A big red-headed woodpecker that can be seen in the trees around campus. Males have a very prominent red cheek, while females have a black one.

Leo Breder ↗

Arapaçu-de-cerrado

Narrow-billed Woodcreeper|Lepidocolaptes angustirostris

Once in its own family (Dendrocolaptidae), exclusive to the Neotropics, woodcreepers are now considered to be a subfamily (Dendrocolaptinae) inside Furnariidae. An insectivore that forages on tree trunks, in bark crevices, and on epiphytes.

07Birds of prey

Leo Breder ↗

Gavião-carijó

Roadside Hawk|Rupornis magnirostris

With some different color morphs, the word carijó is an allusion to the rufous streaks on its belly. Although it preys mainly on insects, it is still probably in the top of many urban food-webs.

Leo Breder ↗

Carcará

Crested Caracara|Caracara plancus

A predator, an opportunist, a scavenger, the Carcará is a widely distributed falcon of the Americas with a very wide dietary niche. It is admired for its adaptability and strength, but seen as evil by some—an icon that catches, kills, and eats whenever it needs to.

Bruno Luka ↗

Carrapateiro

Yellow-headed Caracara|Daptrius chimachima

The common name refers to the habit of individuals of this species to forage for ticks (carrapatos) and other ectoparasites on the backs of cows and horses.

Leo Breder ↗

Coruja-buraqueiro

Burrowing Owl|Athene cunicularia

Small diurnal owl that occurs in open habitats, where it makes burrows in which small groups nest during the night. The same species also occurs in North America, and can breed as far north as the USA–Canada border.

08Mammals

Leo Breder ↗

Capivara

Capybara|Hydrochoerus hydrochaeris

The biggest rodent in the world is also the cutest. On campus, it is commonly found in the Olympic course, but you can also spot it in the banks of the Pinheiros River, by car or by cycling along the bike lane.

Darci Lopes

Sagui-de-tufos-brancos × Sagui-de-tufos-pretos

Common × Black-tufted Marmoset|Callithrix jacchus × penicillata

Despite the Brazilian fauna being famous for its many big and distinct primates, the ones that occur in São Paulo require more habitat than it is available on campus…but we still have our little gremlins. They are probably all hybrids of the introduced white-tufted C. jacchus (native to northeastern Brazil) with the black-tufted C. penicillata (native to the Cerrado of São Paulo, but not the city). Please don’t feed them.

Marcelo Okamoto ↗

Saruê

White-eared Opossum|Didelphis albiventris

You can encounter this opossum crossing streets, power lines, tree branches or a recently installed canopy bridge in the Rua do Matão. In October, the females will be carrying her juveniles around.

09Lizards

Leo Breder ↗

Teiu

Black-and-white Tegu|Salvator merianae

It is an active omnivorous generalist with a diverse diet, encompassing various invertebrates, vertebrates, eggs, carcasses, and fruit. It fulfills multiple ecological roles, acting as a predator, carcass remover, and potential seed disperser.

Bruno Luka ↗

Teiniaguá

Collared Spiny Lizard|Tropidurus catalanensis

According to Brazilian Gaúcho folklore, the Teiniaguá was a beautiful Moorish princess who was brought to South America and transformed by the devil into a lizard with a fiery jewel on her head. She is said to live with her sacristan lover in the Cerro do Jarau crater, where a treasure is rumored to be hidden.
